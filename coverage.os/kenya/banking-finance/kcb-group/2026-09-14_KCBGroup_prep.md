## Snapshot
- KCB Group Plc is a non-operating holding company and East Africa's largest
  bank by assets, headquartered in Nairobi and listed on the Nairobi
  Securities Exchange (NSE: KCB) with roughly 193,981 individual and
  institutional shareholders as of end-2025. It operates KCB Bank Kenya plus
  regional banking units in Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi and South
  Sudan, alongside non-bank subsidiaries — KCB Insurance Agency, KCB
  Investment Bank, KCB Asset Management, KCB Capital and KCB Foundation.
  Led by Group CEO Paul Russo under Chairman Joseph Kinyua.

## Why Now
- H1 2026 (reported 12 Aug 2026): profit before tax up 20.8% to KSh 49.3bn;
  net profit after tax KSh 36.87bn, up 14% y/y; interim dividend raised to
  KSh 3/share (from KSh 2 a year earlier).
- Q1 2026: pre-tax profit KSh 24.43bn, up 15.3% y/y, driven by falling
  funding costs and loan-book growth.
- Mobile loan disbursements up 25% to KSh 314bn in H1 2026; continued
  agency-banking expansion across Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda and Rwanda;
  launched MoFaya, a mobile money/lending joint venture with MTN, in Rwanda.
- Actively finalizing entry into Ethiopia — a target entity has been
  identified, with entry planned before end of 2026. This is the single
  biggest near-term strategic story to raise.
- Regional subsidiaries — notably DR Congo, Rwanda and Tanzania — drove
  76.3% of the Group's FY2025 non-Kenya profit (KSh 20.3bn), underscoring a
  genuine and growing cross-border growth angle beyond the Kenya balance
  sheet.

## Sector Context
- The Central Bank of Kenya has cut its benchmark rate for ten consecutive
  meetings, down to 8.75% (10 Feb 2026) from a 12-year high of 13% in June
  2024; inflation is stable around 4.4%, within the 2.5–7.5% target band,
  leaving room for further easing.
- Average commercial lending rates have fallen to ~14.3% (July 2026) from
  17.2% (Nov 2024), though pass-through varies widely by bank (Citibank
  Kenya ~10.5% vs. Credit Bank ~18.9%) — an uneven-transmission story worth
  probing.
- CBK is shifting the sector from bank-specific base lending rates to a
  more transparent, risk-based, supervision-led pricing framework, and has
  opened consultation on classifying Domestic Systemically Important Banks
  (D-SIBs) with additional loss-absorbing capital requirements. As the
  largest bank by assets, KCB is a likely D-SIB candidate.
- Kenya's major listed banks (KCB, Equity, NCBA) all posted strong H1 2026
  results, with profitability driven more by lower credit impairment and
  cost discipline than by rate-driven margin expansion — a sector-wide
  quality-of-earnings story, not just a KCB one.
